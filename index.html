Chapter 1

Out of Asia, mostly

The cat family dates to the Oligocene. The oldest accepted felid, Proailurus, lived in Europe 25–31 million years ago. But every living cat descends from a much younger ancestor. A 2006 study using 22,789 base pairs of DNA and 16 fossil calibrations put the base of modern cats at about 10.8 million years ago, probably in Asia. The Panthera lineage was the first to branch off. Rising and falling sea levels then let cats cross between continents at least 10 times.

    Dates differ by up to three times depending on method. Dating that includes fossils directly (Tseng 2014) gives much older ages than molecular clocks anchored at a few nodes (Johnson 2006). Treat any single number as a midpoint in a wide range.

    The Tibetan fossil, and its demotion

    Panthera blytheae, a skull from the Zanda Basin in Tibet dated 5.95–4.10 Mya, was described in 2014 as the oldest Panthera and a relative of the snow leopard. It became key evidence for an Asian origin. In 2023, Helmut Hemmer moved it to a new genus, Palaeopanthera, as a more distant relative. debated

    Tseng et al. 2014 Proc R Soc B · Hemmer 2023

    The Laetoli leopard that may be a puma

    Lion- and leopard-like fossils from Laetoli, Tanzania (3.6–3.8 Mya) were long used to calibrate big cat molecular clocks. A 2023 re-study named the lion-sized cat Panthera principialis and assigned the leopard-sized one to an early African Puma. If that holds, the oldest true leopard record is unknown. debated

    Hemmer 2023 Palaeobiodiv Palaeoenv 103:465

    Cheetahs and pumas aren't Panthera

    Both belong to the Felinae subfamily, in a lineage with the jaguarundi. The cheetah branched off about 4.9 Mya. DNA studies suggested cheetah ancestors moved from North America to the Old World, but no Acinonyx fossil has been found in North America. The oldest are from North Africa (about 2.5 Mya). debated

    Johnson 2006 · Dobrynin 2015 · Faurby 2016

    The "American cheetah" was a puma

    Miracinonyx looked like a cheetah but ancient DNA places it next to the puma. Its sprinter's build evolved separately. Nuclear genomes published in 2026 date its split from the puma to about 2.6 Mya.

    Barnett 2005 Curr Biol · Cassatt-Johnstone et al. 2026 (press release)

    Chapter 2

    A family tree that won't sit still

    The five Panthera species split from each other within a few million years. That was fast enough that in 2010 one team noted that no two molecular studies had produced the same tree. Whole genomes have settled one pairing: tiger and snow leopard are sisters. Whether the lion's closest relative is the leopard or the jaguar is still argued.

    Why genes disagree

    Incomplete lineage sorting. When species split quickly, the ancestral population still carries several versions of many genes. Each daughter species keeps a random subset, so by chance a gene can match a more distant cousin. No hybridization is needed.

    Introgression. Hybrids backcross into a parent species and carry DNA across the species line. It leaves a lopsided excess of one conflicting tree and gene copies that are younger than the species split. Tests like ABBA-BABA look for exactly that signal.

    The snow leopard's borrowed mitochondria

    Nuclear DNA puts the snow leopard next to the tiger. Its mitochondrial DNA, inherited only from mothers, groups instead with lion and leopard. A 2016 study of about 63,000 genome-wide markers concluded that an ancient hybrid between the snow leopard and lion/leopard lineages left behind a lion/leopard-type mitochondrion. Large blocks of the X chromosome show the same pattern.

    Li et al. 2016 Genome Research 26:1–11

    Chapter 3

    Thirteen years of big cat genomes

    The first big cat genome, from a 9-year-old male Amur tiger in a Korean zoo, was published in 2013. Early assemblies were thousands of fragments. By 2026 a Sumatran tiger genome had 17 of its 19 chromosomes read end to end. Across all of them, one finding holds: big cat chromosomes have barely changed in 11–15 million years.

    What the genomes revealed

    Diet

    Built for meat

    178 genes show positive selection in the tiger, enriched for muscle filament function (MYH7, TPM4). Cats have lost starch and sugar digestion genes, including the amylases, and GCKR is broken in every cat examined.

    Cho 2013 · Kim 2016

    Altitude

    Snow leopard oxygen

    Snow leopards carry a unique change in EGLN1 (Met39) and changes in EPAS1, two genes in the body's low-oxygen response. Their hemoglobin is not specially adapted, so the adaptation likely works elsewhere.

    Cho 2013 · Janecka 2015 · Yang 2025

    Bottleneck

    The cheetah's narrow past

    Two population crashes, more than 100,000 years ago and about 12,000 years ago, left cheetah genomes almost uniform. Fixed defects in the sperm gene AKAP4 may explain why over 80% of their sperm are misshapen.

    Dobrynin 2015

    Skull

    Jaguar bite

    Jaguar-specific selection on ESRP1 and SSTR4, which shape the skull. The authors link this to the jaguar's massive head and its diet of armored reptiles. interpretation

    Figueiró 2017

    Smell

    Solitary noses

    The solitary tiger has the largest working set of smell and pheromone receptor genes. In the social lion, 68% of one pheromone receptor family (V1R) no longer works.

    Bredemeyer 2023

    Karyotype

    Frozen chromosomes

    Cat genomes have very few duplicated segments (about 1–1.5%, versus about 7% in humans), which may explain why their chromosomes barely rearrange. Not all cats have 38: the ocelot lineage has 36 after a fusion.

    Bredemeyer 2023 · Armstrong 2022

    Chapter 4

    Ancient hybrids and modern ligers

    Big cat species interbred long after they split. The lion lineage shows the most mixing, probably because its huge ancient range overlapped with every other species. Hybridization may even have been useful: two jaguar vision genes sit in regions borrowed from other species and also show signs of natural selection.

    Why ligers are giants and tigons aren't

    Some genes are switched on only from the father's copy and push growth; others, from the mother's copy, hold it back. The leading idea is that lion fathers pass strong growth signals while tigress mothers pass weak brakes, so a liger gets an accelerator with no brakes. A tigon gets the reverse. The pattern is proven in deer mice but has never been measured in ligers. hypothesis

    Vrana et al. 1998 Nat Genet (mice)

    Sterile males, fertile females

    Hybrid big cats follow Haldane's rule: male hybrids are reported sterile, while females can sometimes breed. A 2023 study built separate lion and tiger genomes from a single liger and found that DXZ4, a region of the X chromosome linked to hybrid male sterility in other cats, evolves faster than 99.5% of the genome.

    Bredemeyer et al. 2023 Nat Genet

    One clouded leopard became two

    In 2006, two teams working independently, one with DNA and one with skulls and coat patterns, found that Bornean and Sumatran clouded leopards are a separate species, Neofelis diardi. Whole genomes later dated the split to about 5.1 Mya (3.8–6.5), older than the lion–leopard split.

    Buckley-Beason 2006 · Kitchener 2006 · Bursell 2022

    The most common tree may be wrong

    Lion + leopard is the most frequent tree in the genome, but it is concentrated in regions where borrowed DNA survives best. A 2025 model-based study rejected it as the true species tree. It could not decide whether the jaguar's closest relative is the lion or the leopard.

    Li 2019 MBE · Santos 2025 Syst Biol

    Chapter 5

    Single letters that change a coat

    A "black panther" is not a species. It is a black leopard or black jaguar, and its spots are still there under the dark coat. Melanism has evolved at least four separate times in the cat family, through different mutations in the same pigment pathway.

    Why black leopards cluster in rainforests

    About 11% of leopards are black. They are concentrated in Asia's moist tropical forests and almost absent from open, dry country. On the Malay Peninsula nearly all leopards are black. Camera traps there needed special methods to tell individuals apart, because the spots are hard to see.

    da Silva et al. 2017 PLoS ONE

    The roar is anatomy, not a known gene

    Lions, tigers and jaguars have large, square vocal folds with a fibroelastic pad, a lowered larynx, and an elastic ligament in place of one hyoid bone. Lion roars run about 40–200 Hz. Researchers no longer think the hyoid alone explains roaring, and no study has found genes for it. The snow leopard, a Panthera that doesn't roar, has intermediate vocal folds.

    Weissengruber 2002 J Anat · Klemuk 2011 PLoS ONE

    Two tigers or six? debated

    In 2017 the IUCN Cat Specialist Group cut tiger subspecies to two: mainland P. t. tigris and Sunda P. t. sondaica. A year later, 32 whole genomes split living tigers into six distinct groups (Bengal, Amur, South China, Sumatran, Indochinese, Malayan) sharing an ancestor about 110,000 years ago. The classification is under review.

    Kitchener 2017 Cat News · Liu 2018 Curr Biol

    The cheetah debate

    In the 1980s, cheetahs accepted skin grafts from unrelated cheetahs, proof of near-identical immune genes. Genomes later found diversity up to 50 times lower than in house cats. But a Serengeti field study found predators, not genes, killed most cubs: about 6 of 125 reached independence. How much low diversity harms cheetahs is still argued.

    O'Brien 1985 Science · Caro & Laurenson 1994 · Dobrynin 2015

    Rescuing the Florida panther

    By the early 1990s, inbred Florida panthers had kinked tails, heart defects and undescended testicles, and models gave them a 95% chance of extinction. In 1995, eight female Texas pumas were released. The population roughly tripled, heterozygosity doubled, and the defects became rarer. Some harmful variants came in too, and ongoing management will likely be needed.

    Johnson et al. 2010 Science

    Lions, split two ways

    Lions are now two subspecies: P. l. leo (West and Central Africa and India) and P. l. melanochaita (southern and eastern Africa). The two lineages split about 70,000 years ago. Gir's Asiatic lions grew from 674 in 2020 to 891 in 2025, but they carry almost no genetic variation.

    Kitchener 2017 · de Manuel 2020 PNAS · Gujarat census 2025

    Chapter 8

    What scientists still don't know

    Who is the jaguar's closest relative?

    Lion or leopard. Genome-scale models in 2025 could not tell the two answers apart.

    How old is Panthera?

    Estimates of the five species' common ancestor range from 3.7 to 10.7 million years ago, depending on how fossils are used.

    Where did cheetahs come from?

    DNA hints at North America, but the fossils are all in Africa and Eurasia.

    When did the ancient hybridizations happen?

    None are dated yet. Dates depend on mutation rates, which were only recently measured from big cat pedigrees.

    Why are ligers so big?

    Genomic imprinting is the leading explanation, but it has never been tested in big cats.

    What genes make a roar?

    None are known. Roaring is explained only by the anatomy of the larynx and vocal folds.
